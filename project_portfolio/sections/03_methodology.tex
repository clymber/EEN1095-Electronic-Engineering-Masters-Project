\section{Dataset and Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Dataset and Preprocessing}
Experiments used the ``Link on 8 Cavity PCB with two 10$\times$10 Via-Arrays''
topology from the signal-integrity/power-integrity (SI/PI) Database
\cite{schierholz2021sipi}. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pcb-topology}, six traces
connect ports $P_1$--$P_6$ to $P_7$--$P_{12}$. Table~\ref{tab:pcb-variables}
lists the ten continuous design variables.

    \label{tab:pcb-variables}
    \centering
    \scriptsize
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.27\columnwidth}lp{0.25\columnwidth}@{}}
        \toprule

        \midrule
        \texttt{EPS}        & Relative permittivity
          & \texttt{TAND}      & Loss tangent \\
        \texttt{PITCH}      & Via pitch
          & \texttt{TRACE\_LEN} & Trace length \\

        \texttt{ANTIPADR}   & Antipad radius
          & \texttt{TDIEL}     & Dielectric thickness \\
        \texttt{DISTTL}     & Inter-trace spacing
          & \texttt{TLWIDTH}   & Trace width \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}

The material variables \texttt{EPS} and \texttt{TAND} are dimensionless; all
geometric variables are recorded in mil. During data cleaning, 18 parameter
records without matching Touchstone files were removed, and 609 Touchstone
files without matching design records were ignored. This left 7,030 aligned
designs. Each file was parsed as a complex
$12\times12$ S-matrix at 200 uniformly spaced frequencies from 0.5 to 100~GHz.
Selected-path experiments used positive insertion loss (IL)
\begin{equation}
    IL_{i,j}(f)=-20\log_{10}|S_{i,j}(f)|.
    \label{eq:insertion-loss}
\end{equation}

The 7,030 designs were shuffled using a configurable random seed, set to 128 for the
reported experiments, and split at design level into 60\% training (4,218 designs), 20\%
validation (1,406), and 20\% test (1,406). All 200 frequencies from each design remained
in the same split. Three data representations were then constructed. The point-wise
models expanded each design into 200 frequency rows, with the ten parameters and
frequency as 11 inputs and one or six insertion-loss targets. The curve and
full-S-matrix models instead retained one sample per design, pairing 10 inputs with
respective $200\times6$ insertion-loss and $200\times288$ real/imaginary matrix tensors.
Neither used the frequency-expanded table.

Preprocessing was fitted separately for each model. Point-wise Ridge and neural
models owned their training-fitted standardisation and, where applicable,
polynomial expansion; the random forest used the unscaled inputs. The curve
model standardised the ten design inputs and each of its six target channels
using pooled training-curve points. The full-S-matrix model standardised its
design inputs and scaled each complex entry by one training-set root-mean-square
magnitude shared by its real and imaginary components. Every fitted transform
was applied unchanged to validation and test data. Consequently, no design or
frequency sample contributed statistics across split boundaries. Training
fitted model parameters, while validation results selected hyperparameters and
neural checkpoints. Test designs were held out from fitting, but intermediate
test results were inspected during model development; the reported test
evaluation is therefore retrospective rather than an untouched confirmatory
experiment.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
Selected-path errors were reported as mean absolute error (MAE) in decibels.
Deep-null MAE applies the same error only where the reference insertion loss
exceeds a threshold calculated from the training responses. Complete-matrix
accuracy was measured directly in the complex domain using complex MAE and
normalised root-mean-square error (NRMSE) across every design, frequency and
port pair. The decibel and complex-domain values answer different questions and
were not compared numerically.

For each headline model transition, absolute $IL_{7,1}$ error was first averaged
over the 200 frequencies of each test design. A paired percentile bootstrap then
resampled the 1,406 complete designs 5,000 times and formed the current-minus-
predecessor difference. These intervals quantify variation across the fixed
test designs; they do not include retraining or model-selection uncertainty.
The project-defined $\pm0.10$~dB band aids interpretation of paired intervals;
it is not an engineering acceptance tolerance.

\subsection{Point-Wise Baseline Models}
\label{sec:pointwise-baselines}
For design $n$ at sampled frequency $f_k$, all six baselines received the same
input
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}^{(n,k)}=
    [(\mathbf{u}^{(n)})^{\mathsf{T}},f_k]^{\mathsf{T}}
    \in\mathbb{R}^{p+1},
    \label{eq:pointwise-input}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{u}^{(n)}=[u_1^{(n)},\ldots,u_p^{(n)}]^{\mathsf{T}}$ is the
design-parameter vector, $p=10$, and $f_k$ is recorded in GHz. Scalar Ridge
predicted only $IL_{7,1}^{(n)}(f_k)$; the remaining models predicted
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{y}^{(n,k)}=[
        &IL_{7,1}^{(n)}(f_k),IL_{8,2}^{(n)}(f_k),IL_{9,3}^{(n)}(f_k),\\
        &IL_{10,4}^{(n)}(f_k),IL_{11,5}^{(n)}(f_k),
        IL_{12,6}^{(n)}(f_k)]^{\mathsf{T}}
        \in\mathbb{R}^{6},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:pointwise-targets}
\end{equation}

The first phase tested the linear formulation. Scalar and Vector Ridge fitted
$\ell_2$-regularised least-squares models to standardised inputs, with the
regularisation strength selected by validation MAE. For Vector Ridge, this MAE
was averaged over all six outputs. Multi-output Ridge fits one coefficient
vector per target without coupling the targets; with the common selected
regularisation, its $IL_{7,1}$ component is therefore the same fitted problem as
Scalar Ridge. Their comparison tests output scope rather than additional
predictive capacity.

The second phase added explicit or local nonlinearity while retaining the
six-output task. Polynomial Ridge first standardised the inputs, expanded each
feature independently as
$x_\ell\mapsto[x_\ell,x_\ell^2,\ldots,x_\ell^D]$, standardised the expanded
columns, and
then fitted Ridge regression. The degree $D$ and regularisation strength were
selected jointly by validation MAE. This powers-only map contains no interaction
terms such as $x_\ell x_r$. Random Forest instead fitted an ensemble of regression
trees to the unscaled inputs, allowing local partitions of the joint
design-frequency space; its configured depth and leaf-size candidates were
evaluated using validation MAE.

The third phase tested learned nonlinear features. The neural multilayer
perceptron (MLP) mapped the standardised inputs through rectified linear unit
(ReLU) layers of widths 128, 128, and 64 to a linear six-unit output. Inputs and
targets were standardised using training data, and the Adam optimiser minimised
mean squared error (MSE) in the scaled target space. Learning-rate reduction and
early stopping monitored validation loss, with the best weights restored. The
Polynomial Neural MLP used the same architecture and training procedure after a
fixed fifth-degree powers-only expansion and a second training-fitted feature
standardisation. Comparing these two MLPs therefore isolates the explicit
univariate powers from the learned nonlinear mapping.

A pooled global reference assigned the mean of every training $IL_{7,1}$ value
to all designs and frequencies. A stronger frequency-dependent reference was
also defined from the training designs:
\begin{equation}
    \overline{IL}_{7,1}^{\mathrm{train}}(f_k)=
    \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{train}}}
    \sum_{n\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}}IL_{7,1}^{(n)}(f_k).
    \label{eq:mean-il-reference}
\end{equation}
It predicts this same frequency-dependent mean curve for every validation or
test design and thus measures the value added by conditioning on the design
parameters.

\subsection{Broadband Neural Model}
\label{sec:broadband-neural}
The whole-curve neural model, labelled Curve Neural in the results, replaced
independent design-frequency rows with one sample per design. For $K=200$
frequencies and $q=6$ paths, its target was
$\mathbf{L}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times q}$, where
$L_{k,c}^{(n)}$ is the positive insertion loss of path $c$ for design $n$ at
$f_k$. Thus, the model mapped only $\mathbf{u}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{10}$ to all
six curves in one forward pass; frequency was not an eleventh input feature.
The design variables were standardised using the training designs, while each
target channel was standardised using its pooled training-curve samples.

The decoder was S-TCNN-style rather than an exact reproduction of
\cite{torun2019spectral}. A width-32 dense encoder was projected and reshaped to
a $25\times32$ latent sequence. Three stride-two transposed convolutions, with
32, 16, and 8 channels, expanded this sequence to 200 positions. Each position
was augmented with its normalised frequency and four Fourier sine/cosine pairs,
then processed by a convolutional refinement layer and a linear six-channel
output. This construction lets decoded features interact across neighbouring
frequency positions while retaining an explicit coordinate for each output.

Training used the curve-aware objective
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{curve}}=
    \operatorname{MSE}(\widehat{\mathbf{L}},\mathbf{L})+
    \lambda_{\Delta}\operatorname{MSE}
    (\Delta_f\widehat{\mathbf{L}},\Delta_f\mathbf{L}),
    \label{eq:curve-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_f$ is the first difference along the frequency grid.
$\lambda_{\Delta}$ was calculated from the training targets so that the
reference derivative term contributed 10\% of the point-wise MSE. Validation
experiments compared absent, linear, and Fourier frequency coordinates;
current-size and compact decoders; and point-wise and curve-aware losses. The
compact Fourier curve-aware configuration was retained, and early stopping
restored the epoch selected by inverse-scaled validation MAE across all six
curves. Because this model also changed the data interface, scaling, architecture
and loss, its comparison with the point-wise MLPs does not isolate any one of
those changes.

\subsection{Reciprocal Full-S-Matrix and Physical Diagnostics}
\label{sec:full-smatrix}
The final model extended the target to
$\{\mathbf{S}^{(n)}(f_k)\}_{k=1}^{K}$, where
$\mathbf{S}^{(n)}(f_k)\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}$ and $m=12$. The external data
contract stored the real and imaginary parts of all $m^2=144$ entries as a
$K\times288$ real-valued tensor. Internally, only the
$r=m(m+1)/2=78$ upper-triangle complex entries were predicted, giving 156 real
channels per frequency. The lower triangle was reconstructed as
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{S}_{i,j}^{(n)}(f_k)
    =\widehat{S}_{j,i}^{(n)}(f_k),\qquad i>j,
    \label{eq:reciprocal-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
so every returned matrix was reciprocal by construction rather than by a soft
penalty.

As in the curve model, the interface accepted one design vector rather than the
frequency-expanded table. The standardised design vector was repeated over the
fixed grid and concatenated at each position with 41 frequency features:
normalised frequency, four Fourier sine/cosine pairs, and 32 Gaussian
radial-basis functions. An input projection followed by three width-128 residual
MLP blocks produced the upper-triangle real and imaginary channels. The same
blocks were applied at every frequency position, so the model returned the
whole grid in one pass but did not use the transposed-convolution decoder of
Section~\ref{sec:broadband-neural}.

Each complex entry was scaled by one training-set root-mean-square magnitude
shared by its real and imaginary components. The selected loss combined
normalised complex MSE with a weight-0.1 Huber penalty on off-diagonal
log-magnitude error. Validation complex NRMSE selected the restored checkpoint.
The passivity-loss weight was zero and the loss contained no causality term;
therefore, neither property was enforced during training.

Physical behaviour was evaluated after reconstruction. Reciprocity was checked
using the normalised Frobenius residual between
$\widehat{\mathbf{S}}$ and $\widehat{\mathbf{S}}^{\mathsf{T}}$. A matrix was
counted as passive on the sampled grid when its largest singular value satisfied
$\sigma_{\max}(\widehat{\mathbf{S}})\leq1$. Causality was assessed using a
normalised Hilbert-transform residual between the real and imaginary responses
over the available positive-frequency band. This last quantity is only a
finite-band diagnostic: the data omit direct current (DC), negative frequencies,
and frequencies above 100~GHz, so it cannot establish full-band causality.

For a same-task reference, the training-mean response below was assigned to
every validation and test design and evaluated using the same complex metrics:
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\mathbf{S}}_{\mathrm{train}}(f_k)=
    \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{train}}}
    \sum_{n\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{train}}}\mathbf{S}^{(n)}(f_k).
    \label{eq:mean-smatrix-reference}
\end{equation}
