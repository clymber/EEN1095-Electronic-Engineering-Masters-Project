\begin{abstract}
Printed circuit board (PCB) interconnects introduce broadband loss, reflection
and crosstalk, while repeated electromagnetic simulation can be expensive.
The reviewed approaches either predict selected responses or do not guarantee
physical consistency. This staged investigation considers one PCB topology,
progressing from point-wise regression to whole-curve prediction and a
design-conditioned model of the reciprocal complex 12-port scattering matrix
at 200 frequencies. On designs held out from fitting, the full-matrix model
reduced complex mean absolute error from 0.0804 for a training-mean reference to
0.0686. Reciprocity was guaranteed by construction, and no passivity violations
were observed on the sampled grid; causality remained a finite-band diagnostic
rather than an enforced property. The whole-curve model achieved lower
$S_{7,1}$ insertion-loss error, while sharp nulls remained difficult. The
results establish feasibility beyond a design-independent reference while
separating selected-path accuracy, output scope and physical validity.
\end{abstract}
