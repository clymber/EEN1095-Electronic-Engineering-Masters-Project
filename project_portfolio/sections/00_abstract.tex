\begin{abstract}
Printed circuit board (PCB) interconnects introduce frequency-dependent loss,
reflections and crosstalk. Electromagnetic simulations characterise these
effects accurately but are costly for repeated design-space exploration. This
work develops a staged machine-learning surrogate for one PCB topology,
progressing from regression baselines to curve-aware neural models and a
frequency-conditioned network predicting a reciprocal complex 12-port
scattering matrix from ten design parameters at 200 frequencies. On designs
held out from training, the final model reduced complex mean absolute error from
0.0804 for a training-mean reference to 0.0686, preserved reciprocity exactly,
and produced no passivity violations on the evaluated grid. It predicted a
broader response than the selected six-curve model but had higher $S_{7,1}$
insertion-loss error; the formulations differ beyond output scope. Sharp
transmission nulls remained difficult. These results demonstrate both the
feasibility and limitations of complete scattering-matrix surrogate modelling
for signal-integrity analysis.
\end{abstract}
