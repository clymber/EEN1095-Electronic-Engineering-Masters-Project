% 1. Context + significance
High-speed printed circuit board (PCB) interconnects such as traces, vias and
multilayer transitions exhibit frequency-dependent loss, reflection and
crosstalk. These effects are described by scattering parameters (S-parameters),
from which signal-integrity measures including insertion loss, return loss and
crosstalk can be derived \cite{pozar2011microwave}. Accurate electromagnetic
simulation captures this behaviour but becomes computationally expensive when
many geometric and material variations must be evaluated
\cite{bruns2007numerical}. Machine-learning surrogate models offer a faster
alternative by learning the relationship between design parameters and
simulated responses
\cite{swaminathan2020demystifying,akinwande2024complex}.

% 2. Gap + approach + main conclusion
This work follows a staged workflow: linear and nonlinear baselines establish
reference performance; curve-aware neural models capture broadband structure;
and a frequency-conditioned network predicts the reciprocal complex 12-port
S-matrix from ten design parameters across 200 frequency points. Evaluation on
held-out designs considers predictive error, reciprocity, passivity and
difficult response regions. The experiments show that broader full-matrix
prediction is feasible, but comes with reduced selected-path accuracy.
